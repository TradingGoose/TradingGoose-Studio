### Design Decisions
- A workflow's billing policy is captured once when its root lifecycle is claimed. Retries and nested executions inherit the captured policy rather than re-reading a mutable subscription tier.
- Terminal results are selected durably from competing application, deadline, cancellation, and infrastructure candidates. Background workers reconcile unresolved attempts and operations before projecting one winning result.
- Cancellation closes dispatch atomically before remote cleanup begins. Provider cancellation is retried and observed through durable operation records instead of being treated as proof that remote work stopped immediately.
- Trigger.dev task duration remains a platform safety ceiling, not the billing-tier contract. Billing deadlines are enforced by the application lifecycle so queued and nested semantics remain explicit.
- Private-tier discovery is grant-based. Public catalogs never include hidden tiers, and possessing a code creates an idempotent user access row rather than directly changing a subscription.
- Tier availability and renewal are separate decisions: archived/private visibility and an existing subscription's ability to renew are evaluated through the shared policy instead of ad hoc UI checks.

### Removed or Replaced Items
- Removed `apps/tradinggoose/lib/workflows/queued-execution-cancellation.ts` and `cancelPendingWorkflowExecution()`. Do not restore a separate queued-only cancellation/logging path; use `cancelWorkflowExecutionAtomically()` and the shared lifecycle reconciliation path.
- Removed the global Trigger.dev maximum-duration setting as the product time-limit mechanism. Do not reintroduce one global task duration as a substitute for the captured tier policy; keep platform ceilings separate from billing enforcement.
- Direct provider execution paths that ignored workflow cancellation were replaced with the abort-aware helpers in `apps/tradinggoose/tools/runtime.ts` and durable operation registration. New durable providers must publish remote identity and participate in reconciliation.
- Scattered public/private tier shaping and subscription eligibility checks were folded into `subscription-tier-display.ts`, `tier-availability-policy.ts`, and access-aware functions in `tiers.ts`; do not recreate component-local policy variants.

### Future Branch Guardrails
- Do not recalculate an in-flight execution's deadline from the current subscription. Preserve the claimed root policy and absolute deadline through retries, children, logs, and SDK results.
- Do not write terminal workflow results directly from new executors or background tasks. Submit lifecycle candidates and let terminal arbitration and projections select and publish the winner.
- Do not dispatch new attempts or remote operations after lifecycle dispatch closes. Register and fence durable work through the lifecycle repository before exposing remote operation identity.
- Do not mark provider work cancelled solely because a cancellation request was accepted. Record observations and allow termination reconciliation to prove or retry terminal cleanup.
- Do not expose private tiers through the public catalog or authorize them from client state. Resolve access from persisted user grants on the server.
- Do not display internal tier IDs as the user-facing applied tier. Persist and render the captured `appliedTierName` so historical logs remain understandable after tier edits.
- Keep database migration files generated from the canonical schemas; future schema work should extend `packages/db/schema/workflows.ts` and `packages/db/schema/billing.ts` rather than hand-editing existing migration history.
